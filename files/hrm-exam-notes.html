The Nature of HRM

Introduction · HR Business Partnership · HRM policies · Global environment · Functional HRM · Strategic HRM

Introduction to HRM
HRM = the process of acquiring, training, appraising, and compensating employees, and attending to their labour relations, health/safety, and fairness concerns, so organizational goals are met through people.
  • Nature/Features
    • People-centred, pervasive across every department
    • Continuous process, not a one-time activity
    • Both an art and a science; multidisciplinary (psychology, sociology, law)
    • Aims at individual development + organizational goals together
  • Objectives of HRM
    • Societal — ethical, socially responsible use of HR
    • Organizational — helps org achieve overall effectiveness
    • Functional — HR's contribution matched to org needs
    • Personal — helps employees meet career goals
  • Scope
    • HR planning, recruitment/selection, T&D, appraisal, compensation, welfare, IR, health & safety, grievance handling
HR Business Partnership (HRBP)
HRBP = an HR professional working directly with line managers, aligning HR strategy with business strategy rather than acting as pure admin support.
  • Ulrich's Model — 4 roles
    • Strategic Partner — aligns HR with business strategy
    • Change Agent — manages transformation
    • Administrative Expert — efficient HR processes
    • Employee Champion — voices employee concerns
  • Moves HR from purely transactional → strategic; embeds HR expertise inside business units
HRM Policies
HR Policy = a formal, written guideline defining how the organization manages people-related matters, giving a consistent decision framework.
  • Types
    • Recruitment & selection, compensation & benefits, leave/attendance
    • Training & development, performance management
    • Anti-harassment/POSH, diversity, disciplinary & grievance
  • Characteristics of a good policy
    • Clear & written, consistent with goals/law, flexible, fair & equally applied
HRM in a Globally Competitive Environment
  • Drivers of change
    • Globalization, technology (AI/automation), war for talent, changing workforce demographics
  • IHRM challenges
    • Managing expatriates, cross-cultural training, compliance across countries, standardization vs localization
  • Global staffing approaches
    • Ethnocentric — home-country nationals in key roles
    • Polycentric — host-country nationals manage local units
    • Geocentric — best person regardless of nationality
    • Regiocentric — staffed on a regional basis
Functional HRM
Functional HRM = the traditional, operational view — HR as a support/service department performing specific administrative functions.
  • Operative functions
    • Procurement — planning, recruitment, selection, placement
    • Development — training, career planning, appraisal
    • Compensation — wage/salary admin, incentives, benefits
    • Maintenance — health, safety, welfare
    • Integration — grievance handling, discipline, relations
Strategic HRM (SHRM)
SHRM = linking HR practices with long-term strategic business objectives — HR as a source of competitive advantage, not just a cost centre.

  • Global staffing: Ethnocentric / Polycentric / Geocentric / Regiocentric

Plan, Acquire, Develop, Career Management

Employee Life Cycle (ELC)
ELC = the stages an employee passes through, from being attracted to the company to their eventual exit.
  • Attraction — employer branding
  • Recruitment & Selection — sourcing & hiring
  • Onboarding — induction, orientation
  • Development — training, growth
  • Retention — engagement, compensation, career growth
  • Separation/Exit — resignation, retirement, exit interviews
A-R-O-D-R-S
Human Resource Planning (HRP)
HRP = forecasting future demand & supply of employees and matching the two so the right people are available at the right time.
  • Steps
    • Analyse objectives/environment → Demand forecast → Supply forecast → Gap analysis → Action plan → Monitor & control
  • Importance
    • Right people/place/time, avoids over/understaffing costs, supports succession planning
Recruitment & Selection
  • Recruitment — searching for & attracting a pool of qualified candidates
    • Internal — promotions, transfers, referrals
    • External — job portals, campus, consultancies, ads, LinkedIn
  • Selection — choosing the most suitable candidate(s) from the pool
    • Screening → Preliminary interview → Tests → Selection interview → Reference check → Medical exam → Offer

Competency Management
Competency = an underlying characteristic (knowledge, skill, ability, trait) resulting in effective/superior performance.
  • Types
    • Core/organizational (integrity, teamwork)
    • Functional/technical (job-specific)
    • Managerial/leadership (decision-making, people management)
  • Competency mapping — identifying key competencies per role and assessing employees against them; used in recruitment, training, appraisal, succession
Career & Talent Management
  • Career Management
    • Career planning, counselling, mentoring, succession planning; "career path" = sequence of positions over time
  • Talent Management
    • Attract → identify → develop → engage → retain → deploy high-potential employees
    • 9-box grid — plots Performance vs Potential to classify talent
Managing GIG & Virtual Employees/Teams
  • Gig workers — temporary/freelance/project-based (not permanent employment)
    • Challenges — low loyalty, unclear legal status, inconsistent benefits, retention
    • Strategies — clear contracts/SLAs, timely payment, cultural inclusion, flexible benefits
  • Virtual teams — work interdependently using tech across geography/time zones, little face-to-face contact
    • Challenges — communication gaps, trust-building, monitoring, isolation, time-zone coordination
    • Strategies — collaboration tools, clear KPIs, regular check-ins, virtual team-building, training remote managers

QUICK RECALL — UNIT 2

  • Life cycle: Attract → Recruit → Onboard → Develop → Retain → Separate
  • Recruitment = positive/attracting; Selection = negative/eliminating
  • Kirkpatrick: Reaction, Learning, Behaviour, Results
  • Gig & virtual teams need clear contracts + deliberate trust-building

Engagement, Performance, Compensation, IR, Compliance

Employee engagement · Performance Management · Compensation & Benefits · Industrial Relations · Workplace laws · Employment relations

Employee Engagement — Changing Nature
Engagement = the level of an employee's emotional commitment, involvement, and enthusiasm toward the organization and its goals.
  • Levels
    • Engaged → Not engaged → Actively disengaged
  • Drivers
    • Meaningful work, recognition, growth, trust in leadership, work-life balance, fair pay, psychological safety
  • How it's changing
    • Annual surveys → continuous/real-time "pulse" feedback
    • Broader focus on well-being & mental health
    • Hybrid/remote work needing new engagement tactics
    • Personalized strategies for multi-generational workforce
    • "Employee Experience (EX)" emerging as a broader concept
Performance Management
Performance Management = a continuous process of identifying, measuring, and developing performance, aligned with strategic goals.
  • Cycle
    • Plan (SMART goals) → Monitor/coach → Appraise/review → Feedback → Reward
  • Methods
    • Traditional — rating scale, ranking, paired comparison, checklist
    • MBO — goals set jointly, appraisal on achievement
    • 360-degree — supervisor + peers + subordinates + self
    • BARS — rates specific observable behaviours
    • Assessment centres — simulations to assess potential
  • Rating errors
    • Halo (one +trait inflates all), Horn (one −trait drags down all)
    • Central tendency (everyone "average"), Recency (overweights recent events)
Compensation & Benefits
Compensation = all financial returns and tangible benefits employees receive from the employment relationship.
  • Components
    • Direct — basic pay, DA, bonus, incentives, commission
    • Indirect (Benefits) — PF, gratuity, insurance, paid leave, perks
  • Special groups
    • Executives — ESOPs, perks, performance bonus
    • Sales staff — commission/incentive-based
    • Gig/contractual — project/piece-rate, fewer benefits
    • Expatriates — foreign service premium, hardship allowance
    • Knowledge workers — skill-based pay, retention bonus, ESOPs
Industrial Relations (IR)
IR = the relationship between employers, employees, and their representative organizations, and the mechanisms to regulate it.
  • Parties — Employer/Management, Employees/Trade Unions, Government
  • Objectives — industrial peace, protecting both sides' interests, reducing disputes, improving productivity
  • Trade unions — protect & promote worker interests via collective bargaining, grievance redressal, welfare
  • Collective bargaining — negotiation between employer(s) & union(s) on wages/conditions
  • Dispute forms — strikes, lockouts, gheraos, go-slow
  • Resolution — conciliation → mediation → arbitration → adjudication (labour courts)
Workplace Laws & Compliance
  • Industrial Relations Code — unions, disputes, standing orders
  • Wage laws — minimum wages, payment of wages, equal remuneration
  • Social security — PF, ESI, gratuity, maternity benefit
  • Occupational safety & working conditions
  • POSH Act — prevention of sexual harassment at workplace
Why it matters: avoids legal penalties, protects employee rights, builds trust & reputation.
Employment Relations
Psychological contract = the unwritten mutual expectations between employer and employee, beyond the formal contract.
  • Grievance handling — complaint → discussion with supervisor → escalation to HR/committee → resolution/appeal
  • Progressive discipline — verbal warning → written warning → suspension → termination

QUICK RECALL — UNIT 3

  • Engagement levels: Engaged / Not engaged / Actively disengaged
  • Appraisal: MBO, 360-degree, BARS, Assessment centres
  • Compensation = Direct (pay) + Indirect (benefits)
  • IR parties: Employer – Union – Government · Resolution: Conciliation → Mediation → Arbitration → Adjudication

Technology, HR Analytics, Innovation

HRIS & HR Analytics · HRM Innovations · HRM in SMEs & Service Sector · Org Transformation · DEI · Wellness, Sustainability & Green HR

HRIS & HR Analytics
HRIS = a software system that collects, stores, and processes HR data to support HR decision-making.
  • Functions
    • Employee database, payroll, ATS, performance/leave modules, self-service portals
  • HR Analytics — 4 types
    • Descriptive — what happened
    • Diagnostic — why it happened
    • Predictive — what's likely to happen
    • Prescriptive — what action to take
  • Common metrics — attrition rate, time-to-hire, cost-per-hire, engagement score, absenteeism, training ROI
  • AI in HRM
    • Resume screening & candidate matching, onboarding chatbots
    • Predictive attrition modelling, personalized learning, sentiment analysis
HRM Innovations & Best Practices
  • Flexible/hybrid work, remote & flexi-hours
  • Continuous performance management (vs. annual reviews)
  • Gamification in training & recruitment
  • Employee Experience (EX) design
  • Skills-based hiring over degree-based
  • Self-service HR mobile apps
HRM in SMEs & Service Sector
  • SMEs
    • Informal HR, no dedicated department, owner-driven
    • Challenges — limited budget, few key employees, hard to attract top talent
    • Approaches — outsourced HR/payroll, low-cost cloud HRIS
  • Service sector
    • Employees ARE the product — behaviour = customer experience ("moment of truth")
    • Focus — soft-skills training, emotional labour, service-linked incentives
Organization Transformation & HR Leadership
  • Large-scale change in structure/culture/strategy (digital transformation, M&A)
  • HR role — change management, communication, reskilling, managing resistance
  • CHRO in the C-suite — shapes business strategy, culture, org design
Diversity, Equity & Inclusion (DEI)
  • Diversity — presence of differences (gender, age, ethnicity, disability)
  • Equity — fair treatment & access, correcting systemic disadvantage
  • Inclusion — culture where everyone feels valued & able to participate
  • Why it matters — wider talent pool, better innovation/decisions, employer brand, legal compliance
  • Practices — bias-free hiring, pay-equity audits, mentorship, accessible workplaces, DEI training
Workplace Wellness, Sustainability & Green HR
  • Wellness — EAPs, mental health support, fitness benefits, ergonomic workspaces, flexible leave
  • Sustainability & HRM — aligning HR with ESG/SDGs; embedding sustainability into culture & rewards
  • Green HRM
    • Green recruitment — eco-aware candidates, sustainability employer branding
    • Green training — educating on eco-friendly practices
    • Green performance management — environmental KPIs
    • Green compensation — rewarding responsible behaviour
    • Examples — paperless HR, e-recruitment, energy-efficient offices, remote work
  • Challenges — higher upfront cost, weak leadership buy-in, hard-to-measure ROI, resistance to change

QUICK RECALL — UNIT 4

  • HR Analytics: Descriptive → Diagnostic → Predictive → Prescriptive
  • DEI = Diversity (mix) + Equity (fairness) + Inclusion (belonging)
  • Green HRM touches recruitment, training, appraisal, and compensation
